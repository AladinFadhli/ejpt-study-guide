# Reporting

The deliverable of a real penetration test is the report, not the access. The eJPT tests whether you can interpret findings, and reporting is a genuine professional skill worth building early.

## Why reporting matters

A client can't act on access they can't see. The report is how findings become fixes. In the real profession, the quality of your report *is* the quality of your work as far as the client is concerned — a brilliant compromise explained badly is a failed engagement.

For the eJPT specifically, the practical exam expects you to understand and interpret what you found — the reporting mindset (evidence, impact, clarity) is exactly that skill.

## The one habit that makes reporting easy

**Take notes as you go, not at the end.** Every host, port, service, credential, finding, and screenshot, recorded when you find it. A report is mostly assembled from good notes. People who skip note-taking spend the end of the exam trying to reconstruct what they did — and miss things.
